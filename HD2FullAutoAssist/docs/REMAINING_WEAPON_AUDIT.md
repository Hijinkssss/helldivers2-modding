For “fires once when held,” the charge/release weapons cannot be treated as ordinary press-to-shot semis: some holds maintain charge without firing; Accelerator releases a native burst; Meltagun discharges a charged cycle while held. For “automatically repeats,” none of the captured five establishes an indefinite repeated-shot/beam train suitable for generic Cadence Control. Finite recordings do not prove every possible mode or native behavior absent. Unknowns above remain explicit.

Displayed SEMI/BOLT/PUMP/charge labels are not used as proof. The captures do not establish whether every displayed label is misleading, and mechanically correct action names can coexist with different input semantics. Eruptor's independently verified native hold-to-repeat is the concrete counterexample; it remains supported as Cadence Control.

## What remains necessary

Four nonbeam candidates share the unresolved input integration and readiness/completion gates. They are not blocked merely by Meltagun. Current `native_fire` only owns ordinary trigger-8 repeat mappings, preserves original bytes and restores them; it has no proven charge-release/re-press adapter. A diagnostic GetAsyncKeyState sample of left mouse is independent of processed Fire but is not a universal active Fire binding.

The next offline work must establish the binding/evaluator source and exact native completion/permission predicates. Accelerator needs its whole burst index/count/recovery path, and Meltagun needs its actual beam entity/consumer. Only then can a narrowly scoped adapter and a relevant live observation test those named gates. Do not invent readiness from plateau durations or rename the existing semantic model as implemented support.

The 31 already-supported identities remain enabled under their existing policies. Eruptor 27/28/32, Commando 120/240, AMR SPECIAL, switching, OFF restoration and the approved HUD have offline regression coverage. The final private RC cannot be declared complete with these five activation gates unresolved.
